Most books die at sixty per cent.

Not at the idea, and not at the first chapter. At the point where writing stops being exciting and becomes work, with nobody to hand it to. That is the gap Author Venture exists to close.

Who we are

A small team of editors, writers, and production people.

We run a publishing house’s process and hand the author the keys at the end.

Author Venture is a book services company. We write, edit, design, publish, and produce children’s books for authors who want professional standards without signing away what they have made.

Internally we work the way a good publishing house works: a real editorial process, real production standards, honest scheduling, and someone whose job is to say “not yet” when a manuscript is not ready. The difference is who is in charge. You decide what the book is. We make sure it gets finished, and finished properly.

We are a young company, and we would rather say so than pad this page with numbers nobody can check. What we will not compromise on is the standard of the work: every manuscript goes through a defined editorial process, every file is produced to retail print specification, and every schedule is one we have actually costed rather than one that sounded reassuring on a call.

Work is staffed by discipline rather than by whoever is free. Editors are matched to genre, illustrators to the style you choose, and production to the format you are printing. One named project manager holds the whole thing together and stays with your book from the first call to the live listing.

How we work

Four rules we do not bend.

01 · Ownership

The book is yours, permanently

Copyright, byline, ISBNs, cover files, and retailer accounts stay in your name. If you left us tomorrow you would take a complete, working book with you, and nothing would be withheld to make leaving difficult.

02 · Honest scope

We will talk you out of things

If your manuscript needs a structural edit we will not sell you a proofread. If it is ready to publish we will not invent an editing round. Selling the wrong service wins one invoice and loses the next three books.

03 · No outcome promises

Bestseller is not a service

We can promise professional writing, real editorial rigour, correct distribution, and a book that meets retail standards. Nobody can promise sales, and we will not price a promise we cannot keep.

04 · Confidentiality

Your draft goes nowhere

NDA before the first page, access-controlled storage, no portfolio use without written consent. A good deal of our best work will never appear on this website, and that is exactly how it should be.

Plain English

The words the industry uses on you.

Publishing hides a lot behind vocabulary, and confused authors overpay. Here is the jargon you are most likely to meet, defined without spin.

Trim size Production
The finished page dimensions. 6 × 9" is the default for non-fiction, 5 × 8" for novels. It changes your page count, and therefore your print cost.
Bleed Production
Artwork extended past the trim line so no white slivers appear when the page is cut. Usually 0.125". Forgetting it is the most common reason a cover file is rejected.
Metadata Retail
Title, subtitle, description, categories, and keywords. It decides which searches your book appears in, and it is the single most neglected part of self-publishing.
BISAC code Retail
The standard subject classification retailers shelve by. The right code puts you in a category you can rank in; the wrong one buries you against textbooks.
Wide distribution Retail
Listing beyond Amazon: Ingram, Apple, Kobo, Barnes & Noble, libraries. It makes your book orderable everywhere, which is not the same as stocked everywhere.
Print-on-demand Production
Copies printed as they are ordered. No garage full of stock, no upfront print run, slightly higher unit cost, and effectively no inventory risk.
Widows and orphans Typesetting
A lone line stranded at the top or bottom of a page. Invisible until you see it, then impossible to unsee. Fixed at the proofreading stage.
Front and back matter Structure
Everything that is not the story: title page, copyright page, dedication, acknowledgements, author bio. Small, fiddly, and the part most self-published books get visibly wrong.
From vision to publication

What the working relationship feels like.

  1. 01

    You are never guessing

    A dated schedule arrives with your quote. You know what is due, when, and what you will be holding at each milestone.

  2. 02

    You keep the final say

    Every editorial change is a suggestion you accept or reject. We will argue our case once, clearly, and then it is your book and your call.

  3. 03

    You are not billed by surprise

    Revisions are in the quote. If scope genuinely changes we tell you before doing the work, never afterwards when the book is half-finished and you have no leverage.

  4. 04

    You leave with everything

    Source files, print-ready PDFs, EPUB, layered cover artwork, ISBNs, and account logins. Complete handover, and no dependency on us afterwards.
